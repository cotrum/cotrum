work.

My research interests lie primarily at the intersection of quantum algorithms and scientific computing. Prior work has consisted of algorithmic work toward the binary closest vector problem using HPCs and quantum computing (DQI), exploring QML and quantum kernels for cybersecurity applications, and using VQEs for chemical simulation. For all of these I am interested in circuit optimization and routing for NISQ computers. See more below.

research

Future of Computing Summer Intern — National Security Agency Summer 2026

Co-developed the blocked fast Walsh–Hadamard transform (BFWHT), an exact set-covering decoder for the low-weight binary closest vector problem, with CUDA/GPU implementations benchmarked on A100-class hardware. Built the end-to-end Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) research codebase with interchangeable decoders and reversible resource estimation, and executed on a 156-qubit IBM QPU.

Quantum Machine Learning Researcher — RPI School of Science Summer 2025
  • Designed a Qiskit quantum-kernel/SVM pipeline for network intrusion detection, achieving 85%+ accuracy on RPI's 127-qubit IBM Quantum System One. Built the reproducible QML-NIDS codebase and first-authored the resulting AAAI QIML proceedings paper.
  • Work expanded into cross-university projects involving the use of QML for phishing and malware detection, employing a kernel-optimized version of the original QML NIDS workflow.
OTHER RESEARCH PROJECTS 2024 – Present
  • Quantum chemistry research--ongoing work at RPI with Dr. Osama Raisuddin on exploring circuit optimization via constant-depth Pauli exponential decomposition for use in molecular simulation with variational quantum eigensolvers.
  • QML research in collaboration with Dr. Brian McDermott at RPI and the Naval Nuclear Laboratory on evaluating the NISQ performance of quantum methods using 3-SAT and DQI.
  • Studied hybrid quantum-classical transformers for the traveling salesman problem alongside graduate researchers at the Chinese University of Hong Kong.
  • (2023-2024)--cybersecurity research with Dr. Brian Callahan at RPI--Developed augmented-reality CTF challenges in steganography, DNS poisoning, and MFA bypass; coauthored a CISSE journal article and ASIA proceedings paper on generative AI and cybersecurity pedagogy.

publications & presentations

Towards Efficient Low-Weight Decoding: A Blocked Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transform

  • Cotrupi, M. L., Gutti, M., Ralph, B., & Sandberg, K. (2026). FCSI 2026 final research report, National Security Agency.
  • At n=64, m=2,097,152, t=8, the BFWHT reduced estimated decoding operations by 732× versus optimized Gray-code enumeration, and reduced estimated logical qubits for DQI by 688× on a 64×64 benchmark.

Towards Practical Quantum Kernels for Network Intrusion Detection
